Our rooms

Booth A

Lead voice booth

Acoustically isolated, with a large-diaphragm condenser mic and a screen for picture-synced takes. Used for lead roles and narration.

Booth B

Ensemble booth

Fits up to four actors facing one another, so group scenes and crowd reactions sound natural.

Mix room

Mix and mastering

Calibrated monitors, 5.1 and stereo delivery, and a treated room for final loudness checks.

Equipment

Typical setup. Replace with your actual gear list.

A typical recording day

10:00Director briefs the cast on scene, tone and character arcs
10:30Warm-up and mic check; first rehearsal with the picture
11:00Recording takes, scene by scene, with live lip-sync checks
14:00Retakes for any line the director or client flags
16:00Editor cleans and syncs the day’s takes
18:00Rough cut shared with the client for notes

How dubbing works

Six steps from your first file to final delivery.

  1. Send files and brief

    Share your video, a script if you have one, and your requirements.

  2. Translate and adapt

    Language experts turn the dialogue into natural Hindi that fits the lip movements.

  3. Casting

    You hear 2–3 voice auditions for each main character.

  4. Recording

    Studio sessions with a director guiding every take.

  5. Mix and sync

    Voices, music and effects are blended with the original video.

  6. Review and delivery

    We apply your feedback and deliver final files with separate tracks.
